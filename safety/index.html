Stay safe

Safety tips for jobseekers and employers

Most people on Job Search PH are honest. These simple habits keep it that way for everyone.

For jobseekers

  • Never pay to get a job. No real employer asks for a processing, training, medical, uniform or "reservation" fee. Under the Kasambahay Law, household workers can't be charged placement fees.
  • Keep your IDs and money safe. Don't send ID photos, bank, GCash or OTP codes before an interview, and never send money to an employer.
  • Check the pay. Every job here must pay at least the legal minimum. Ask the employer how pay is computed and when it is paid.
  • Talk here first. Use the chat on Job Search PH until you trust the employer. It keeps a record if something goes wrong.
  • Meet safely. Go to the first interview in daytime, tell family the address, or bring someone you trust.
  • Get it in writing. Ask for a written contract with pay, hours, days off and benefits (SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG once covered).
  • Be suspicious of offers that are too good. Very high pay, "start today, no interview", or jobs abroad from someone without a DMW license are red flags.
  • Show up or say so. If you can't make an interview, message the employer as early as you can. It protects your reputation on the site.

For employers

  • Meet before you hire. Interview in person or by video call, and check references from past employers.
  • Check documents yourself. Ask for a valid ID and barangay or NBI clearance during the interview, not by chat to strangers.
  • Never ask applicants for money or deductions to apply or be hired. It's against our rules and the Kasambahay Law.
  • Be clear and honest. Post the real pay, hours, days off and duties. Changing them after people apply wastes everyone's time.
  • Follow the law. Pay at least the minimum wage, give a weekly rest day, register household workers with SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG, and give a written contract.
  • Keep applicants' details private. Use them only for hiring and don't share them.
  • Reply to applicants. Even a quick "not this time" helps people move on.
  • Close the post when it's filled so jobseekers don't keep applying.
In danger or being forced to work? Call 911 or the PNP right away. For labor complaints, contact DOLE Negros Occidental, or the DOLE hotline 1349.
